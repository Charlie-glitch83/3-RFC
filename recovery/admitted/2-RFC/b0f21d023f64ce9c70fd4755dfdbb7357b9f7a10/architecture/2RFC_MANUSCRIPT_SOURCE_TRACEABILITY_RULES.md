## Source classes

Every load-bearing planned claim, equation, state field, theorem target, or boundary must be assigned one of these classes:

- `DIRECT`: stated or proved directly in an identified Allan Edward manuscript section, theorem, definition, lemma, appendix, or addendum.
- `INHERITED`: established upstream from direct manuscript material and received through a sealed parent packet.
- `REPRESENTATION`: a manuscript scaffold, proxy, packet, computational screen, adapter, or numerical illustration that may guide representation but does not itself prove the stronger module claim.
- `METADATA`: source-lock, reproduction, naming, supersession, or computational-trace material; it does not add physical law.
- `EXTERNAL`: public-data, benchmark, or standard-model comparison material; generative use is forbidden outside Module P.
- `NEW-DERIVATION`: science required by the plan that the identified manuscripts do not yet prove. It must be derived from the sealed parent and authorized direct sources without presenting it as already established.
- `FORBIDDEN`: obsolete, superseded, failed, target-derived, publicly fitted, or scope-incompatible material.

A broad phrase such as “applicable manuscript science” is insufficient for a load-bearing claim. The implementation must name the source and anchor.

- Public measurements, catalogues, standard constants, public codes, and conventional models may enter only Module P as comparison or explicitly declared benchmark material. They cannot generate Modules A-O or Q.


When the source supports only a scaffold, proxy, finite audit, or boundary, the implementation must say so.

When no manuscript proves a required step, the status is `NEW-DERIVATION`, not “already established.”

## Failure rule

A missing source anchor, unsupported promotion, contradiction among manuscript sources, or use beyond a manuscript claim boundary stops the affected claim.

It does not authorize filling the gap with general knowledge, conventional physics, public best fits, Wolfram curated models, or retuning.
