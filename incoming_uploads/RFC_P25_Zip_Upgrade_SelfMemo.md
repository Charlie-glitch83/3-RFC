# 1. The central danger



If I write the better version badly, I can fail in two opposite ways.



## 1.1 Failure mode A: repeat Presentation 25's old overclaim



Presentation 25's abstract and module summaries often speak as though the simulation modules already match or reproduce major public observables: Planck CMB observations, baryon asymmetry bounds, neutrino freeze-out, particle mass hierarchies, gravitational-wave echoes, and observer decoherence. That was rhetorically powerful, but too strong relative to the actual status. It made internal symbolic/proxy modules sound like external likelihood calculations. - RFC solves inflation.

- RFC computes reheating.

- RFC computes QCD and electroweak transitions.

The ZIP files repeatedly block exactly those claims. The better Presentation 25 must obey the blocks.


The ZIP files strengthen this sequence but do not naturally tell it. I must not let ledger language replace explanation. The new version needs both:

The better paper should read like a theory paper, not a spreadsheet, but every claim should be ledger-safe.

Better wording to self:



> The triad is not a prefatory metaphor. It is the generative source of the kernel, the reason the kernel can fan out across modules, and the rule that every module must inherit the same no-retune packet.

- Keep the idea that identity can be modeled as attractor stabilization.

- Route all empirical observer/neural claims through Z2 external-analysis boundaries.

That single form allowed the paper to explain why very different domains could be treated as different modal bases of the same recursion. This is the heart of the paper. The better version must keep it.



But the ZIP files force a correction: the kernel cannot remain a free symbolic operator with old free constants. It must be downstream of the deterministic closeout packet:

The kernel survives, but its authority changes. It is no longer an attractive general function. It is the mathematical fan-out of a frozen triadic packet.
