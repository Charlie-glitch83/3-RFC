
# Self-Memo: How to Write RFC Documents and Preprints Without Sounding Like AI

**Prepared for:** future drafting of Recursive Fractal Cosmology (RFC) manuscripts, preprints, reports, and source-facing documents  
**Purpose:** build a public-source-informed writing doctrine for producing documents that read as authentic scientific work rather than machine-polished, generic, inflated, or evasive prose.  
**Core rule:** this is **not** a guide to evading AI detectors. It is a guide to preserving human scientific authorship, intellectual responsibility, concrete reasoning, and disciplinary credibility.

---

## 0. Executive instruction to myself

When writing the next RFC preprint, do **not** try to make the prose “undetectable.” That is the wrong target. The correct target is to make the prose **traceable, accountable, technically specific, logically staged, and visibly authored by a human mind making decisions under uncertainty**.

The problem with AI-sounding prose is not merely vocabulary. It is a deeper pattern:

1. It often explains before it has earned the explanation.
2. It often smooths conflict instead of identifying it.
3. It often sounds comprehensive while hiding missing dependencies.
4. It often uses symmetric paragraph shapes even where the argument is asymmetric.
5. It often overuses intensifiers, abstract nouns, and polished transitions.
6. It often removes the author’s actual judgment calls.
7. It often replaces “what we did” with “what the framework represents.”
8. It often substitutes panoramic synthesis for testable claims.
9. It often produces clean prose that lacks provenance.
10. It often fails to leave a paper trail of how claims were derived, constrained, blocked, or revised.

For RFC, this is especially dangerous because RFC already contains abstract concepts: triad, recursive lattice, symbolic ontology, closure, kernel, modal basis, projection bridge, boundary ledgers. If these are written in a machine-polished style, reviewers will read them as speculative or generated, even when the underlying architecture is internally organized. The writing must therefore **make the work feel earned**.

The key writing goal:

> Write like a scientist who knows exactly where the theory is strong, exactly where it is provisional, exactly what was computed, exactly what was not computed, and exactly why the current paper stops where it stops.

## 1. Public-source synthesis: what the literature and policies imply

### 1.1 Publisher policies agree on one thing: human accountability is non-negotiable

Nature Portfolio states that large language models do not satisfy authorship criteria because authorship carries accountability, and that LLM use should be documented in the Methods section or an appropriate alternative section when it goes beyond copy editing. Nature also distinguishes AI-assisted copy editing from generative editorial work and says authors must remain accountable for the final text.

Elsevier states that AI tools must not substitute for human critical thinking, expertise, and evaluation; authors must review and verify accuracy, comprehensiveness, impartiality, and sources; authors must disclose AI tool use when required; and authors remain responsible and accountable for the contents of the work. Elsevier also warns that AI-generated references can be incorrect or fabricated.

**Writing consequence:** do not hide behind polished language. In the RFC manuscript, the authorial stance must be: “Here is the derivation chain; here are the results; here is what these results do and do not establish; here is what remains open.”

### 1.2 AI detectors are not a sound writing target

Public research and reporting repeatedly show that AI-detection tools have limitations, including false positives, false negatives, domain sensitivity, and vulnerability to paraphrase or prompt variation. Some studies find practical detectors unreliable across domains or models, and reporting has raised concern about false accusations. This means the goal should never be “beat a detector.”

**Writing consequence:** the manuscript should not be optimized around superficial detector signals. Avoiding “AI voice” should be framed as a matter of scientific clarity, authenticity, and accountability, not as a game against classifiers.

### 1.3 AI-generated text has recurring linguistic tendencies

Survey literature on AI-generated text reports tendencies such as a more formal and impersonal style, lower lexical diversity, smaller vocabulary, and repetitive text. Public discussion and empirical work have also identified overrepresented AI-associated words and phrases in recent academic or professional discourse, including terms such as “delve,” “intricate,” “meticulous,” “pivotal,” “realm,” “tapestry,” and similar polished generalities.

**Writing consequence:** do not merely ban a list of words. Instead, remove the habit underneath those words: inflated abstraction, prefabricated transitions, symmetrical praise, and vague significance claims.

### 1.4 Good scientific prose is reader-directed, not ornament-directed

Scientific writing conventions vary across disciplines, but useful public guidance converges on clarity, structure, concise presentation, appropriate evidence, and logical organization. IMRAD exists because readers need to locate the problem, method, result, and interpretation quickly. Plain-language principles also emphasize audience, headings, topic sentences, concise sentences, familiar vocabulary where possible, active constructions when useful, and logical ordering.

**Writing consequence:** RFC must not lead with grand claims. It must lead with a map, definitions, frozen assumptions, procedures, outputs, and boundaries.

### 1.5 AI-assisted writing is acceptable only when authorship is visibly human

Ethically acceptable AI-assisted writing depends on human intellectual control: the human supplies the original conceptual contribution, decides the claims, verifies references, checks computations, revises language, and accepts responsibility. If AI output displaces the author’s actual thinking, the work becomes fragile.

**Writing consequence:** when drafting RFC, use AI as a scaffold, not as a ghostwriter. The final text must contain the author’s real structure, judgments, caveats, and priorities.

## 2. The difference between “human scientific voice” and “AI-polished voice”

### 2.1 AI-polished voice tends to sound like this

It often says:

> This section delves into the intricate interplay between recursive structures and emergent cosmological phenomena, highlighting the pivotal role of the triadic framework in shaping a robust and comprehensive understanding of existence.

Problems:

- “delves into” is filler.
- “intricate interplay” is vague.
- “emergent cosmological phenomena” is too broad.
- “pivotal role” asserts importance without showing mechanism.
- “robust and comprehensive understanding” sounds promotional.
- The sentence contains no operation, no boundary, no result, no dependency.

### 2.2 Human scientific voice should sound like this

Instead write:

> In this paper, the triad is used only as a dependency structure. CIF denotes the modal input space, QV denotes the compression/damping channel, and RFL denotes the realized output state. The first calculation fixes the internal parameter packet from this structure without observational targets. Later modules may test the packet, but they may not retune it.

Strengths:

- Defines terms by function.
- States paper scope.
- Names the first calculation.
- Establishes no-retune order.
- Separates derivation from testing.
- Uses plain verbs.
- Does not overclaim.

### 2.3 AI-polished prose hides the author; human scientific prose exposes the author’s decisions

A scientific preprint should make the reader see the decisions:

- Why this parameter is frozen.
- Why this module is downstream.
- Why this result is called internal rather than external.
- Why this comparison is blocked.
- Why this proxy is not a detection claim.
- Why a figure is schematic rather than empirical.
- Why a legacy statement is retained, rewritten, or deprecated.

The human voice is not “casual.” It is **accountable**.

## 3. The ethical boundary: do not write to evade detection

This report must not be used to disguise authorship or misrepresent AI involvement. The correct use is:

- improve clarity;
- remove generic filler;
- sharpen claims;
- preserve the author’s conceptual intent;
- document AI assistance if required;
- make the reasoning and sources auditable;
- reduce machine-like homogeneity;
- make the paper more useful to human reviewers.

The wrong use is:

- hiding substantial AI authorship;
- fabricating a human process that did not occur;
- adding fake imperfections to trick readers;
- inventing citations;
- paraphrasing generated prose until detectors fail;
- using “humanization” tools to mask copied AI output;
- submitting text whose claims the author cannot defend.

A credible RFC preprint can acknowledge tool assistance if required, but it must make clear that the scientific decisions, conceptual architecture, numerical values, source hierarchy, and claim boundaries are human-controlled.

## 4. Why RFC is at special risk of sounding AI-generated

RFC has several features that can accidentally resemble AI-style writing if mishandled:

1. It uses broad synthesis across cosmology, quantum theory, field theory, symbolic ontology, computation, and cognition.
2. It uses high-level nouns such as emergence, recursion, coherence, lattice, ontology, field, closure, projection, and memory.
3. It has a triadic conceptual structure that can easily become slogan-like.
4. It contains many modules, which can encourage list-like prose.
5. It has an ambitious scope, which can sound inflated unless claim boundaries are explicit.
6. It has metaphysical origins, which must be translated into scientific functions.
7. It contains simulation and proxy language, which must be separated from external empirical proof.
8. It has prior preprints with strong explanatory flow but sometimes strong claim language.
9. It has newer closeout ledgers with high discipline but less narrative power.
10. It is being developed through AI-assisted work logs, which makes provenance discipline essential.

Therefore, the next preprint must be written with unusually tight control over:

- claim status;
- source hierarchy;
- module dependency;
- numerical provenance;
- citations;
- theorem vs simulation vs proxy vs speculation;
- visual explanation;
- tone.

## 5. The core style doctrine for RFC

### 5.1 Prefer functional definitions over evocative definitions

Weak:

> QV is the fertile nothingness from which recursive collapse initiates the birth of structure.

Better for a scientific preprint:

> QV denotes the compression channel: the part of the model that supplies damping, decay, entropy-gradient terms, and branch-selective actualization.

Both mean related things, but the second is defensible in a technical manuscript.

### 5.2 Prefer dependency chains over panoramic claims

Weak:

> RFC unifies cosmology, particle physics, quantum gravity, and cognition through the universal principle of recursive fractal emergence.

Better:

> The present paper does not claim external unification of these domains. It specifies a shared internal dependency chain. Each downstream domain receives the same frozen kernel packet; whether the packet survives domain-specific external tests is treated as a separate boundary question.

### 5.3 Prefer claim labels over adjectives

Weak:

> The result is remarkable, robust, and deeply suggestive.

Better:

> The result is an internal consistency pass. It is not an observational fit. It becomes externally testable only after the public-data handoff in Section X.

### 5.4 Prefer numbers with provenance over narrative confidence

Weak:

> The deterministic closure procedure yields a stable and highly coherent parameter set.

Better:

> The deterministic closure procedure yields the frozen packet \(\delta=4.6692\), \(\alpha=0.0256831\), \(\nu=0.00420784\), \(\epsilon=0.000108071\), \(\lambda=0.489442\), \(n_{closure}=18\), and \(n_{full}=40\). No observational target is used in deriving this packet.

### 5.5 Prefer admitted incompleteness over totalizing language

Weak:

> This framework provides a complete route to physical constants.

Better:

> The current bridge preserves internal dimensional identities. It does not yet derive CODATA constants in SI units. That step is assigned to the external calibration lane.

## 6. The anti-AI prose checklist

Before finalizing any RFC section, ask:

1. Does this paragraph contain a result, dependency, definition, or decision?
2. If not, why is it here?
3. Does every broad term have a functional role?
4. Does every module state its input packet?
5. Does every validation claim state whether it is internal, proxy, finite-suite, theorem-ready, or external?
6. Does every external-sounding claim cite public data or explicitly defer to a future public-data run?
7. Does every figure state whether it is schematic, simulated, projected, or empirical?
8. Does every numerical value have source provenance?
9. Does the paragraph avoid boilerplate transitions?
10. Does the prose expose human judgment rather than generic synthesis?

If a paragraph cannot pass this checklist, revise it.

## 8. Human scientific prose has friction

AI-like prose is often too smooth. Human scientific prose often contains productive friction:

- a limitation after a claim;
- a reason for excluding an interpretation;
- a term whose definition narrows rather than expands the idea;
- a table that says “not yet validated”; 
- a paragraph that says “this result should not be overread”; 
- a sentence that admits two modules are not directly comparable;
- an explicit difference between internal units and public physical constants.

This friction makes a paper more credible.

For RFC, use friction deliberately:

> The projection bridge is useful because it preserves internal dimensional identities. It is also easy to overstate. The projected values are not SI constants. We therefore report them as internal RFC units and reserve comparison with CODATA values for the external calibration lane.

That sentence sounds human because it contains a judgment, a risk, and a boundary.

## 9. Section-by-section writing doctrine for a scientific preprint

### 9.1 Title

Bad AI-style title:

> Recursive Fractal Cosmology: A Comprehensive Framework for Understanding the Intricate Interplay of Existence, Emergence, and Universal Structure

Better title:

> Recursive Fractal Cosmology: A Deterministic Triadic Closure Framework

or:

> Recursive Fractal Cosmology: A Triad-Derived Kernel and No-Retune Validation Protocol

A title should say what the paper does, not how sweeping the theory feels.

### 9.2 Abstract

The abstract must have five moves:

1. Problem.
2. Method.
3. Frozen inputs.
4. Results/outputs.
5. Boundaries.

Avoid:

- “This paper delves into...”
- “We present a groundbreaking...”
- “The results offer profound insights...”
- “A comprehensive framework is established...”

Use:

- “We define...”
- “We derive...”
- “We test...”
- “We report...”
- “We do not claim...”

Example abstract skeleton:

> We formulate RFC as a triad-derived recursive kernel rather than a fitted phenomenological model. CIF is treated as the modal input space, QV as the compression/damping channel, and RFL as the realized output structure. From this dependency order we derive a frozen internal parameter packet without observational targets. We then propagate the packet through source-coupled closure, dimensional projection, and downstream boundary screens. The resulting pipeline separates internal consistency from external validation. The present paper reports the internal closure structure and specifies the public-data runs required for cosmology, BBN, CP/EDM, gravitational-wave, and particle-sector comparison.

### 9.3 Introduction

The introduction must not begin with metaphysical scope. Begin with a scientific problem:

- Many theoretical frameworks fit parameters upstream.
- Cross-domain models often lack a clean dependency order.
- RFC previously had strong explanatory structure but needed a no-retune architecture.
- This paper supplies that architecture.

The triad should appear as a functional dependency structure, not as mythic language.

Example move:

> The question addressed here is not whether a triadic ontology is philosophically attractive. The question is whether the triad can be made operational: can three roles—modal input, compression, and realized structure—generate a fixed kernel packet that is then propagated downstream without retuning?

### 9.4 Theory section

The theory section must define all primitives by operation.

- CIF: input/modal possibility space.
- QV: compression/damping/asymmetry channel.
- RFL: realized lattice/output/memory geometry.

Do not say “CIF is infinite potential” unless immediately translated into the scientific role.

### 9.5 Methods section

The Methods section should be the least poetic part of the paper.

It must answer:

- What is fixed?
- What is derived?
- What is computed?
- What is downstream?
- What is not allowed?
- What public data is not yet used?

Use short procedural paragraphs.

### 9.6 Results section

Do not narrate results as destiny.

Bad:

> These results demonstrate the powerful coherence of RFC across scales.

Better:

> These results show that the same frozen packet can be propagated through the tested internal modules without retuning. This is an internal consistency result. It does not establish agreement with public observations.

### 9.7 Discussion section

The Discussion should interpret, not inflate.

Use this sequence:

1. What was established.
2. What was not established.
3. Why the distinction matters.
4. Which legacy claims are softened.
5. Which external tests come next.

### 9.8 Conclusion

Do not end with “opens new horizons.” End with the exact next step.

Example:

> The next required step is a public-data implementation of the boundary lanes defined here. Until those runs are complete, RFC should be described as an internally closed triadic kernel framework with specified external validation requirements, not as a completed empirical replacement for standard cosmology or the Standard Model.

## 10. The sentence-level rules

### Rule 1: Make the grammatical subject the actual actor

Weak:

> The emergence of structure is facilitated by the recursive compression of modal potential.

Better:

> QV compresses CIF modal content into RFL structure.

### Rule 2: Replace abstract verbs with operational verbs

Avoid overuse of:

- enables
- facilitates
- underscores
- highlights
- represents
- serves as
- embodies
- captures
- reflects

Prefer when accurate:

- fixes
- derives
- maps
- propagates
- constrains
- excludes
- compares
- normalizes
- projects
- decomposes
- audits
- bounds
- reports
- fails
- passes

### Rule 3: Put the claim before the explanation

Weak:

> Through a recursive process involving the modal interaction of CIF and the compressive action of QV, the RFL emerges as a stabilized lattice capable of preserving memory-like structure.

Better:

> RFL is the model’s realized output state. It is produced when the QV compression channel acts on CIF modal content and retains the result as recursive structure.

### Rule 4: Keep uncertainty precise

Bad hedging:

> The results may potentially suggest a possible route toward a deeper understanding.

Good hedging:

> The result is limited to internal closure. It does not test external data.

### Rule 5: Avoid ornamental balance

AI often writes sentences in balanced pairs:

> not only X, but also Y
> both A and B
> from X to Y
> at once P and Q

These are fine when needed, but they become artificial when every paragraph uses them.

### Rule 6: Do not use “we” as decorative authority

Use “we” when the paper actually performs an action:

- We define.
- We derive.
- We compute.
- We compare.
- We restrict.
- We report.

Do not use “we” for vague gestures:

- We explore the intricate relationship.
- We illuminate the profound significance.
- We uncover a rich tapestry.

### Rule 7: Do not end paragraphs with vague significance

Weak final sentence:

> This highlights the profound importance of recursive fractality in the structure of reality.

Better final sentence:

> This is why the packet is frozen before any downstream module is evaluated.

## 11. Paragraph-level rules

### 11.1 Every paragraph must have one job

A paragraph should do one of these:

- define a term;
- state a problem;
- describe a method;
- report a result;
- explain a consequence;
- set a boundary;
- compare to prior work;
- motivate a figure;
- transition between sections.

If a paragraph does more than two of these, split it.

### 11.2 Put the topic sentence in plain language

Bad:

> The intricate interdependence of modal possibility and recursive actualization reveals a central organizational feature of the theory.

Better:

> The triad enters the model as a dependency order.

Then explain.

### 11.3 End with consequence, not drama

Bad:

> This reveals the deep unity of the recursive cosmological vision.

Better:

> For this reason, downstream modules may test the packet but may not alter it.

### 11.4 Use asymmetry when the science is asymmetric

AI prose often makes everything balanced. RFC should not. Some results are central; others are provisional. Some modules are closed internally; others are external-boundary lanes. Some old claims survive; some must be retired.

Good paragraph shape:

- Sentence 1: Claim.
- Sentence 2: Evidence or source.
- Sentence 3: Boundary.
- Sentence 4: Consequence.

Example:

> Module N V2 solves an internal projection problem, not an external calibration problem. It preserves dimensional identities after the frozen packet is propagated through the projection bridge. The projected constants are therefore reported in RFC internal units. They should not be compared directly with SI constants until the Module S/T calibration lane is executed.

## 12. Claim-boundary language that sounds scientific rather than AI-generated

Use exact labels.

### 12.1 Internal consistency

> This is an internal consistency result: the same frozen packet is propagated through the module without retuning.

### 12.2 Proxy result

> This is a proxy screen: it checks whether the reduced RFC quantity moves in the expected direction, not whether it matches the full physical observable.

### 12.3 External-data boundary

> This comparison is deferred to an external public-data run. No observational likelihood is used in the current derivation.

### 12.4 Theorem-readiness

> This module is theorem-ready only in the limited sense that the relevant definitions and invariants have been fixed. A formal proof is not supplied here.

### 12.5 Blocked claim

> The current result does not justify that claim. We therefore list it as blocked pending the required external calculation.

### 12.6 Legacy reconciliation

> The earlier formulation is retained as motivation but not as the active derivation method.

These phrases sound human because they make hard distinctions.

## 13. Citation behavior: where AI-sounding papers fail

AI-assisted papers often fail by citing too broadly, citing fake sources, or citing sources that only loosely support a claim. For RFC, citation discipline must be strict.

### 13.1 Citation rules

1. Every public fact gets a source.
2. Every numerical constant gets a source or an internal provenance label.
3. Every external-data dataset gets a source and version/date.
4. Every claim about standard cosmology, particle physics, BBN, gravitational waves, or EDM limits gets a public-source citation.
5. Every claim about RFC internal modules gets an internal project-source citation or appendix pointer.
6. No citation should be added because it “looks right.”
7. No source should be cited if it has not been opened or verified.
8. Reference metadata must be checked manually.

### 13.2 Citation red flags

- DOI that does not resolve.
- Journal title mismatch.
- Paper title too perfectly tailored to claim.
- Author list too generic.
- Citation supports a general topic but not the specific sentence.
- Citations clustered at paragraph ends without sentence-level mapping.
- References added by AI without verification.

### 13.3 RFC citation style

Use two classes of references:

- **External references**: public scientific literature, CODATA/NIST, Planck, PDG, LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA, BBN datasets, etc.
- **Internal provenance references**: project preprints, transfer logs, ZIP ledgers, module closeouts, simulation logs.

The manuscript should explicitly separate them.

## 14. Figure and table style: visual human credibility

AI-sounding preprints often have generic diagrams with impressive labels but no operational content. RFC must avoid this.

### 14.1 Every figure must answer one question

Bad figure title:

> The Recursive Fractal Architecture of Existence

Better:

> Dependency order used in the no-retune RFC pipeline

### 14.2 Required figure captions

Every figure caption should state:

- what is shown;
- whether it is schematic, simulated, projected, or empirical;
- what inputs are fixed;
- what should not be inferred.

Example:

> Figure X. Schematic dependency order of the RFC rebuild. The figure shows the allowed flow from triadic definitions to the frozen Module G packet and downstream boundary screens. It is not an observational data plot and should not be interpreted as a fit.

### 14.3 Tables should include status columns

For RFC, tables should include columns like:

- Module
- Input packet
- Output quantity
- Claim status
- External data required
- Current limitation

This prevents “AI-like” sweeping claims by forcing every row to state its boundary.

## 15. Humanizing without casualizing

The goal is not to make a preprint chatty. It should remain scientific. Human voice comes from specificity, not slang.

### 15.1 Human but still scientific

> The older RFC pipeline allowed empirical assimilation too early. That made the theory difficult to distinguish from a flexible fitting scheme. The rebuild fixes this by deriving the packet before any observational comparison.

### 15.2 Too casual

> The older version kind of had the fit problem, so we cleaned that up.

### 15.3 Too AI-polished

> The previous architecture presented challenges related to the intricate balance between empirical assimilation and theoretical consistency, motivating a more robust and transparent recalibration of the framework.

The best version is direct, not casual.

## 16. RFC-specific before/after rewrites

### Example 1: Triad definition

AI-like:

> The CIF, QV, and RFL form a profound triadic ontology that captures the intricate interplay between infinite possibility, vacuum fluctuation, and emergent structure.

Scientific:

> The triad is used as a three-role dependency structure. CIF denotes the modal input space, QV denotes the compression and damping channel, and RFL denotes the realized output structure.

### Example 2: Kernel claim

AI-like:

> The recursive kernel provides a powerful bridge between symbolic ontology and physical observables, enabling a unified treatment across many domains.

Scientific:

> The recursive kernel is the map used to propagate the frozen packet into domain-specific modal bases. Each module changes the basis function, not the upstream packet.

### Example 3: Presentation 25 upgrade

AI-like:

> By integrating the ZIP closeout ledgers, Presentation 25 can be transformed into a more rigorous and comprehensive module architecture.

Scientific:

> The ZIP ledgers upgrade Presentation 25 by adding a no-retune rule, claim-status labels, and external-data handoff requirements to each module.

### Example 4: External validation

AI-like:

> The results align with several observational domains and point toward a promising empirical future.

Scientific:

> The current results define boundary lanes. Alignment with public observations remains untested until the specified datasets and likelihood codes are applied.

### Example 5: Legacy reconciliation

AI-like:

> The previous preprints form a rich foundation that is harmonized through the present rebuild.

Scientific:

> The earlier preprints supply definitions, motivation, and module forms. Their fit-forward language is superseded by the frozen-packet dependency order used here.

## 17. How to preserve explanation power without sounding promotional

The older RFC materials had strong explanatory flow. The risk is that removing poetic language can make the paper dry. The solution is not to restore metaphysical rhetoric. The solution is to write explanations as mechanistic sequences.

### 17.1 Bad explanation

> Reality emerges through the timeless dance of nothingness and infinity.

### 17.2 Overcorrected dry version

> The model contains three components.

### 17.3 Best scientific explanation

> RFC starts from three roles that cannot be collapsed into one another. A modal space is needed to specify possible states. A compression channel is needed to select and damp those states. A realized structure is needed to preserve the selected result. We call these roles CIF, QV, and RFL, respectively.

That preserves explanation while becoming scientific.

## 18. How to write uncertainty in RFC

### 18.1 Avoid vague optimism

Do not write:

> This may open the door to future breakthroughs.

Write:

> This leaves one unresolved task: the projected values must be calibrated against public constants before any external claim can be made.

### 18.2 Avoid fake humility

Do not write:

> While more work is needed, the framework is highly promising.

Write:

> The present result is limited to the internal pipeline. It does not evaluate observational likelihoods.

### 18.3 Avoid vague caveats

Do not write:

> There are limitations that should be considered.

Write:

> The limitation is specific: the BBN screen uses reduced proxies and does not solve a full nuclear reaction network.

## 19. How to write methods so they sound human

A methods section sounds human when it includes the actual constraints and decisions.

### 19.1 Method paragraph template

> We begin with [fixed input]. We compute [quantity] using [procedure]. We then pass [output] to [next module]. At no stage do we use [excluded target]. This restriction is imposed because [reason].

### 19.2 RFC example

> We begin with the deterministic Module G packet. We compute the source-coupled residual decomposition in Module R using the same packet. We then pass the resulting closure quantities into the dimensional projection bridge. At no stage do we use observational targets to adjust the packet. This restriction is imposed to keep derivation and validation separate.

This is the opposite of AI vagueness.

---

## 20. How to write results so they sound human

A results section sounds human when it reports the result and immediately frames the permissible inference.

### 20.1 Results paragraph template

> The calculation returns [result]. This supports [specific inference]. It does not support [nearby stronger inference]. We therefore use the result as [claim status].

### 20.2 RFC example

> The closure audit returns a QV-dominant source decomposition. This supports the internal interpretation that compression and entropy-recoil channels dominate the residual structure. It does not establish that QV is a measured physical substance. We therefore use the result as an internal source-coupling audit, not as an external detection claim.

## 21. How to write discussion so it sounds human

A discussion section should not be a victory lap. It should be an interpretive audit.

### 21.1 Discussion paragraph template

> The main contribution of this paper is [specific contribution]. Its importance is [reason]. Its limitation is [specific limitation]. The next test is [specific external or formal step].

### 21.2 RFC example

> The main contribution of this paper is the replacement of fit-forward RFC modules with a deterministic no-retune dependency chain. This matters because it separates internal derivation from external validation. The limitation is that most downstream comparisons remain boundary screens rather than public-data likelihood runs. The next test is to execute the cosmology, BBN, particle-sector, and gravitational-wave lanes against their specified external datasets.

## 22. How to use AI during drafting without losing human authorship

### 22.1 Acceptable uses

- Organizing notes.
- Finding inconsistencies.
- Suggesting section order.
- Checking for repeated phrases.
- Producing a list of claim boundaries to verify.
- Helping turn rough notes into clean prose when the author supplies the claims.
- Copy editing for grammar, punctuation, and readability.
- Comparing a draft against a checklist.

### 22.2 High-risk uses

- Asking AI to generate scientific claims.
- Asking AI to invent citations.
- Asking AI to write the final argument from scratch.
- Accepting AI’s mathematical exposition without checking it.
- Letting AI choose the claim strength.
- Letting AI decide whether a result is validated.

## 25. The public-data principle

If a claim sounds external, it must be tied to public data or explicitly deferred.

Examples:

### Cosmology

Do not write:

> RFC matches cosmological observations.

Write:

> The current cosmology lane specifies the required public-data comparison. It does not yet report a full likelihood run against CMB, BAO, or supernova datasets.

### BBN

Do not write:

> RFC resolves BBN tensions.

Write:

> The current BBN screen uses reduced proxies. A full reaction-network calculation is required before any abundance claim can be made.

### Particle physics

Do not write:

> RFC derives the Standard Model.

Write:

> The current particle-sector map is an internal frozen-packet retest. It does not replace the Standard Model Lagrangian or provide a full renormalized EFT.

### Gravitational waves

Do not write:

> RFC predicts observable echoes.

Write:

> The current gravitational-wave lane defines a ringdown/echo boundary test. It does not report a detection.

## 29. The “reviewer trust” protocol

A skeptical reviewer trusts papers that do the following:

- define terms before using them;
- separate derivation from validation;
- state limitations near results, not only at the end;
- cite sources accurately;
- avoid grandiosity;
- show reproducible steps;
- avoid hiding weak spots;
- explain why old claims changed;
- distinguish internal model units from physical units;
- make future tests concrete.

A skeptical reviewer distrusts papers that:

- use too many high-level nouns;
- present a theory as complete too early;
- treat proxies as observations;
- use generic visuals;
- overuse “framework” language;
- claim unification without showing a dependency map;
- write in a uniformly polished tone;
- cite everything broadly but nothing precisely;
- avoid hard falsifiers.

|---|---|

## 31. The “specificity ladder”

When a sentence sounds AI-like, climb down the ladder from vague to specific.

### Level 5: Promotional abstraction

> The theory offers a profound new understanding of cosmic emergence.

### Level 4: General theory claim

> The theory models emergence through recursion.

### Level 3: Functional claim

> The theory models emergence as compression of modal input into realized structure.

### Level 2: Formal claim

> CIF supplies modal input, QV supplies damping/compression, and RFL stores the output state.

### Level 1: Procedural claim

> We derive the frozen packet from the CIF/QV/RFL dependency order and propagate it downstream without retuning.

In preprints, use Level 1 or 2 most of the time. Use Level 3 in introductions. Avoid Level 4 unless tied to formal definitions. Avoid Level 5.

## 32. The “reader expectation” version of RFC prose

Readers expect old information before new information. Do not introduce a new term at the end of a sentence and then switch topics. Use sentence positions deliberately.

Weak:

> A deterministic closure packet is generated through recursive triadic constraints that define the operational relation between modal possibility, compression, and stabilized structure.

Better:

> The triad defines three roles: modal possibility, compression, and stabilized structure. We impose these roles as recursive constraints. The constraints generate the deterministic closure packet.

This version is less ornamental and easier to follow.

## 34. Introduction template

> Many speculative cross-domain theories fail because they introduce flexible parameters before defining a strict dependency order. RFC previously developed a rich explanatory architecture, but several early formulations allowed empirical assimilation too close to the derivation stage. The present paper removes that ambiguity. We treat the triad as an operational dependency structure rather than a metaphor: CIF defines modal input, QV defines compression and damping, and RFL defines realized output. The first task is to derive the internal packet from this structure alone. The second task is to propagate that packet through downstream modules without retuning. The third task is to state which comparisons remain internal, which are proxy screens, and which require public-data validation.

This introduction sounds human because it admits the prior weakness and states exactly how the new paper fixes it.

## 35. Methods template

> The calculation is staged to prevent target leakage. First, the triadic roles are fixed. Second, the closure packet is derived from internal constraints only. Third, source-coupled residuals are audited without modifying the packet. Fourth, dimensional projection is performed in internal units. Fifth, downstream modules receive the packet as input. No downstream result is allowed to alter the upstream packet.

Use this style throughout.

- Say what was fixed.

# Appendix B. RFC Claim Boundary Catalogue

## Internal derivation
